Knowledge Pulse is Document360's built-in analytics dashboard that gives you a clear picture of how readers interact with your content. Use it to spot high-performing articles, discover where engagement drops off, and find the search queries that aren't returning useful results — all in one place.

## Accessing Knowledge Pulse

To open Knowledge Pulse, click **Knowledge Pulse** in the left sidebar of the Document360 portal. The dashboard loads with your most recent data and defaults to the last 30 days.

## Overview Dashboard

The top of the dashboard displays a set of summary metrics that give you an at-a-glance health check of your knowledge base:

| Metric | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| **Total article views** | How many times readers opened an article in the selected period |
| **Unique visitors** | The number of distinct readers who visited your knowledge base |
| **Average time on page** | How long readers typically spend reading an article |
| **Articles with no views** | Articles that received zero traffic — candidates for archiving or promotion |
| **Search volume** | Total number of searches performed by readers |

Use the **date range filter** in the top-right corner to switch between the last **7 days**, **30 days**, **90 days**, or a **custom range**. Narrowing the window helps you evaluate the impact of a recent content push; widening it reveals longer-term trends.

## Article Performance

Click the **Articles** tab to see a table of every published article and how it is performing. The table is sorted by views by default, so your most-read content appears first.

Each row in the table includes the following columns:

- **Article title** — the name of the article, linked to its detail view
- **Views** — total number of times the article was opened
- **Avg. time on page** — mean reading time in minutes and seconds
- **Thumbs up** — count of positive helpfulness ratings
- **Thumbs down** — count of negative helpfulness ratings
- **Last updated** — the date the article was most recently published

Click any article title to open its **detail view**, where you can see a day-by-day view trend, the ratio of positive to negative ratings, and reader comments if comments are enabled.

## Search Analytics

Click the **Search** tab to understand how readers use your knowledge base's search function. This view surfaces three key data points:

- **Top search queries** — the terms readers search for most often, along with how frequently each query appears
- **No-result searches** — queries that returned zero matching articles
- **Click-through rate** — the percentage of searches where a reader clicked at least one result

No-result searches are the most actionable metric here. Each entry in that list represents a reader who needed help and walked away empty-handed. Review this list regularly and treat each recurring query as a direct request for new content.

## Filtering and Exporting

At the top of any Knowledge Pulse view, use the filter controls to narrow the data:

- **Workspace** — focus on a specific project or workspace
- **Language** — filter to a single language variant of your knowledge base
- **Date range** — set a precise reporting window

When you have the view configured the way you want, click the **Export** button to download the current table as a CSV file. Exported files include all visible columns and respect any active filters.

<Tip>
Build a weekly review habit: every Monday, open the **Search** tab and sort by no-result searches. The queries at the top of that list are your most urgent content gaps — turn the top three into new articles or FAQ entries before the week is out.
</Tip>

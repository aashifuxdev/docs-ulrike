Feedback Manager is the central place in Document360 where all reader signals come together. Whether a reader clicked thumbs down on an article, left a written comment, or had an unsatisfying conversation with the Eddy AI chatbot, those signals appear here — giving you a prioritized queue of documentation improvements to work through.

## Article Feedback

At the bottom of every article on your public knowledge base site, readers see a prompt that asks **"Was this article helpful?"** with a thumbs up and thumbs down option. To enable this prompt, go to **Settings → Knowledge base site → Read receipt** and turn on the article feedback toggle.

Once enabled, every rating is recorded and aggregated in **Feedback Manager → Articles**. The tab shows each article alongside its total thumbs-up count, thumbs-down count, and overall helpfulness ratio. Articles with a high proportion of negative ratings are the ones most in need of your attention.

## Viewing Article Comments

When article comments are enabled, readers can leave written feedback directly on an article page. To view those comments, navigate to **Feedback Manager → Articles** and click into any article row to expand its detail panel.

From this view you can:

- Read every comment left on the article
- See the date and time each comment was submitted
- **Reply** to individual comments directly from the Feedback Manager interface — your reply is visible to the reader on the knowledge base site

Use comments to get qualitative context that thumbs ratings alone cannot provide. A reader who explains exactly what confused them is giving you a free content audit.

## Eddy AI Chatbot Feedback

The **Feedback Manager → Eddy AI** tab shows a log of every conversation your readers have had with the Eddy AI chatbot. For each conversation you can see:

- The **question** the reader asked
- The **answer** the chatbot provided
- The reader's **rating** of that answer (thumbs up or thumbs down)

Sort by low-rated answers to find the conversations where Eddy AI fell short. In most cases, a poor answer means the underlying source content is incomplete, out of date, or doesn't exist yet — all of which you can fix directly in the knowledge base editor.

## Filtering Feedback

Use the filter controls at the top of any Feedback Manager tab to focus on the signals that matter most right now:

- **Date range** — narrow to a specific reporting window
- **Workspace** — view feedback for a single project
- **Language** — isolate feedback from a specific language variant
- **Feedback type** — show only **positive** or only **negative** feedback

Combining a short date range with a negative-only filter is a fast way to see what broke after a recent content update.

## Acting on Feedback

Treat Feedback Manager as an ongoing editorial queue rather than a one-time report. Here is a reliable improvement loop:

1. **Identify** articles with negative feedback or low helpfulness ratios.
2. **Open** the article in the Document360 editor.
3. **Improve** the content — use the built-in Eddy AI assistant to suggest rewrites, or revise the article manually.
4. **Republish** the article and clear the feedback item once the fix is live.

For chatbot failures, the fix is the same: find the source article the chatbot draws from, update or expand that content, and republish. The chatbot's answers improve automatically as its source material improves.

<Tip>
Sort the **Articles** tab by **Thumbs down** in descending order to instantly surface the articles most urgently needing improvement. Start your editing session with the article at the top of that list.
</Tip>

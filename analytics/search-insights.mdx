Search Insights gives you a window into what your readers actually want to know. By analyzing the queries they type, where those queries succeed, and — critically — where they return nothing useful, you can make precise, evidence-backed decisions about which content to create or improve next.

## What Search Insights Shows

Search Insights lives inside **Knowledge Pulse → Search** tab. The view surfaces three categories of search data:

- **Top search queries** — the terms readers search for most, ranked by frequency, along with the click-through rate for each query (the percentage of searches that led to a reader opening an article)
- **Zero-result searches** — queries that matched no articles at all; readers who ran these searches left your knowledge base without finding anything
- **Low-engagement searches** — queries that returned results but where readers didn't click any of them, suggesting the results were irrelevant or unconvincing

Together, these three categories tell you not just what readers are looking for, but how well your knowledge base is meeting them.

## Zero-Result Searches

Zero-result searches are the highest-priority signal in Search Insights. Each one represents a reader who came to your knowledge base with a specific need and left with nothing. Treat this list as a direct, unfiltered list of content requests from your audience.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Review the list of no-result queries">
    Open **Knowledge Pulse → Search** and scroll to the zero-result searches section. Sort by frequency to see which queries are failing the most readers.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Identify recurring patterns">
    Look for clusters of related queries — for example, multiple variations of the same question worded differently. Group these into a single content gap rather than treating each query as a separate task.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create a new article or FAQ entry for each gap">
    Open the Document360 editor and create a new article that directly answers the most common query in the cluster. Use the exact words readers typed as keywords in the title and introduction to ensure the new article surfaces in future searches.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Publish the article">
    Publish the new article and note the date. Give it at least one full week of traffic before evaluating its impact.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Monitor the query in next week's search report">
    Return to Search Insights the following week and check whether the query has moved from the zero-result list to the top queries list with a healthy click-through rate. If it still shows zero results, verify that the article is published and that its title and content include the search term.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Improving Search Relevance

Document360 search uses a combination of **full-text matching** and **semantic matching**, which means it looks for both exact keyword matches and conceptually related content. Even so, there are several things you can do to make sure the right articles rise to the top:

- **Add relevant keywords to article titles and introductions.** Search ranking weighs titles heavily — if a reader's query doesn't appear anywhere near the top of your article, the article may rank lower than expected.
- **Use the glossary to define common terms.** When readers search for a term you've defined in Document360's glossary, the glossary entry appears prominently in results and helps orient readers before they dive into a longer article.
- **Give articles clear and specific titles.** Vague titles like "Overview" or "General information" are difficult for the search engine to match confidently. Prefer titles like "How to reset your account password" or "Understanding role-based access control."

## Custom Search Suggestions

For your most important articles and key terms, you can pin specific articles to the top of search results. Go to **Settings → Knowledge base site → Article settings** and look for the **Featured search results** option. Here you can map a search query to a specific article so that readers who type that query always see your preferred article first.

This is especially useful for:

- High-stakes topics like billing, cancellation, or account security
- Terms that are unique to your product and might not match article titles literally
- Onboarding queries where you want to funnel new users to a specific getting-started guide

<Tip>
Filter Search Insights by **language** to spot content gaps that are specific to non-English versions of your knowledge base. A zero-result search in your French or German site often points to an article that exists in English but hasn't been translated yet.
</Tip>

<Note>
Search data in Search Insights reflects activity on your **public knowledge base site** and the **embedded KB widget**. Searches made inside the Document360 portal by your internal team are not included in this data.
</Note>

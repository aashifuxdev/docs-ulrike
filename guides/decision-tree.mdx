Decision trees let you replace long, conditional troubleshooting articles with an interactive, guided experience. Instead of asking readers to scan through blocks of text looking for their specific scenario, you present them with a clear question at each step and let them navigate to the resolution that matches their situation.

## What is a decision tree?

A decision tree is a visual, branching guide where each node presents a question or step, and the reader selects a path to move forward. The tree continues branching until the reader reaches a final resolution or answer node.

Decision trees are especially effective for:

- **Troubleshooting flows** — Walk users through diagnostic steps to identify and fix a problem.
- **Diagnostics** — Help users identify which product, plan, or configuration applies to their situation.
- **Step-by-step onboarding** — Guide new users through a setup process that varies depending on their choices.

## Creating a decision tree

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Decision tree">
    Click **Decision tree** in the left sidebar of the Document360 portal.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create a new tree">
    Click **New decision tree** and enter a name for the tree.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Edit the root node">
    You start with a single root node. Click the node to enter your opening question or introductory statement — for example, *"Is the issue affecting all users or just one?"*
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add branches">
    Click the **+** icon below a node to add branches. You can use Yes/No options or enter custom answer labels to suit your flow.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Build out the tree">
    Each branch leads to a new node. Continue adding questions and branches until every path ends at a resolution node — a final answer or recommended action for the reader.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save the tree">
    Click **Save** to preserve your work. You can return to edit the tree at any time before or after publishing.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Publishing a decision tree

Decision trees publish independently from articles and do not need to be embedded in an article to be accessible.

To publish a decision tree, open it and set its status to **Published**. Once published, it appears on your knowledge base site under a dedicated **Decision trees** section in the navigation. You can also link directly to a specific tree from any article by copying the tree's public URL and inserting it as a hyperlink.

## Editing nodes

Click any node in the tree editor to open its editing panel. From there you can:

- **Edit the node text** — Update the question, answer label, or resolution message.
- **Add media** — Insert an image to provide visual context alongside the node text.
- **Link to an article** — Add a reference to a related article so readers can dig deeper into a topic without leaving the decision tree flow.

## Embedding in articles

You can embed a decision tree inline within any article so readers encounter the interactive flow in the context of your written content.

In the article editor, type the slash command `/decision-tree` and select the tree you want to embed from the list. The tree renders as an interactive component directly within the article body.

<Tip>
  Decision trees work particularly well for IT troubleshooting guides (for example, network connectivity issues), account setup flows where the steps differ by user role, and returns or refund policy guidance where the outcome depends on the customer's situation.
</Tip>

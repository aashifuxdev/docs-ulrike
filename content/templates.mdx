Templates let you define a reusable article structure once and apply it every time you create a new article. Instead of starting from a blank page, your writers begin with the right headings, sections, and placeholder text already in place — making documentation faster to produce and more consistent across your knowledge base.

<Note>
  Templates are workspace-specific. A template you create in one workspace is not available in other workspaces under the same account.
</Note>

## What are templates?

A template is a pre-built article structure stored in your workspace. It can contain any combination of block types — headings, paragraphs with placeholder text, callouts, tables, code blocks — arranged exactly as you want them to appear in finished articles. When a writer picks a template while creating a new article, Document360 copies the entire structure into the new article, leaving the writer free to fill in the real content.

## Creating a template

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Templates">
    In the left sidebar, click **Content tools**, then select **Templates**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Start a new template">
    Click **New template** in the top-right corner.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name and describe the template">
    Enter a clear **Template name** and an optional **Description** so your team knows when to use it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Build the content structure">
    Use the full block editor to add headings, placeholder text, callouts, and any other blocks that belong in this article type. Placeholder text like *"Describe the problem this article solves"* helps writers know what to write in each section.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save the template">
    Click **Save**. The template is now available to all writers in the workspace.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Using a template

When you click **New article** anywhere in the knowledge base, a modal appears asking how you want to start. Choose **Select a template**, browse the list, and click the template you want. Document360 copies the template's full block structure into the new article. From there, you edit the article just as you would any other — the template content is not linked back to the source.

## Editing and deleting templates

To update an existing template, go to **Content tools > Templates** and click the template name to open it in the editor. Make your changes and click **Save**. Updates apply to future articles only — articles already created from the template are not affected.

To delete a template, open **Content tools > Templates**, find the template in the list, click the **⋮** (more options) menu on its row, and select **Delete**. Deleting a template does not affect articles that were previously created from it.

<Tip>
  Templates work especially well for repeating content types. Consider creating a template for each of these:

  - **Release notes** — version, date, new features, bug fixes, known issues
  - **API endpoint docs** — endpoint, method, parameters, request/response examples
  - **How-to guides** — overview, prerequisites, step-by-step instructions, next steps
  - **FAQ entries** — question heading, direct answer, related links
</Tip>
